title: Accessibility
description: How Seintje works with screen readers, keyboards and reduced motion.
---

## Announcements

The toaster keeps two visually hidden live regions. New toasts, and toasts whose content is
replaced, are read out as "title. description". Errors use the assertive region, everything
else the polite one. Override this per toast with `important: true` or `important: false`.

Because announcements are separate from the animated markup, screen readers are not flooded
while toasts move around.

## Markup

- The toaster is a `<section>` with an accessible name (`label`, default "Notifications") that
  also names the hotkey.
- Toasts are list items, labelled by their title and described by their description.
- The close and action controls are real `<button>` elements. Nothing interactive is nested
  inside another interactive element.
- A collapsed toast's body is `inert`, so hidden buttons cannot be focused.

## Keyboard

| Key | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> | Moves focus to the newest toast. Change it with the `hotkey` option, or turn it off with `hotkey: false`. |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves between toasts and their buttons. A focused toast opens. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Dismisses the focused toast and moves focus to the next one. When none is left, focus goes back to where it was before the hotkey. |

## Time

Every auto-dismiss timer of a toaster pauses while a toast is hovered or focused, and while the
page is hidden. When it resumes, the toast gets the time it had left, not a fresh start. Use
`duration: null` for messages people must act on.

## Reduced motion

With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, shapes and layout jump to their new size instead of
springing, the gooey stretch and swipe drift are skipped, and only short fades remain. The
toaster's `motion` option overrides the system setting: `"reduce"` always reduces, `"full"`
never does.
